How it works

Where the weather comes from

Trafalgar Square has no weather station of its own. The closest official Met Office station is London, St James's Park (WMO station 03770), about 600 metres away. Every verdict and every scene on this site is built from observations at that one station. The app never uses forecasts, never randomises the weather, and never quietly switches to another station.

  • Primary source: Met Office Weather DataHub, Land Observations API. Hourly observations for the last 48 hours, including the Met Office weather type code, temperature, visibility, wind and pressure. Each run first asks DataHub for the station nearest Trafalgar Square and refuses to publish anything unless it is St James's Park.
  • Snow confirmation: nearby official present-weather sensors. St James's Park has no sensor that can tell snow from rain, so the app also reads the nearest stations that do: London City Airport (about 13 km east, aviation weather reports via NOAA's Aviation Weather Center), Northolt (about 21 km) and Heathrow (about 22 km), both from the Met Office. Their reports only ever support a St James's Park measurement, never replace it.
  • Secondary source: Met Office UK Land Surface Observations on the AWS Open Data Registry (bucket met-office-land-observations-data). One-minute observations used to back-fill missed runs, add precipitation totals and cross-check DataHub temperatures. The station name and coordinates in every file are checked before use.

A GitHub Actions job fetches the data every hour, applies the rules below, and commits the results as JSON files in the data/ folder. Every day's file includes the raw observations it was based on, so any verdict can be checked.

The snow rule

Each day is a UK calendar day (midnight to midnight, UK local time), so days are 23 or 25 hours long when the clocks change.

  1. SNOW if either:
    • St James's Park reports a snow weather code (22 to 27) in any hour of the day, or at the following midnight; or
    • a nearby sensor reports falling snow, and within an hour of that report St James's Park itself measured precipitation with the temperature at 2 °C or below. Snow seen nearby while central London stayed dry or mild does not count.
  2. UNCONFIRMED if snow cannot be ruled out: hours with no weather code at St James's Park when it was 6 °C or colder that no nearby sensor can settle, or fewer than half the hours arrived. If precipitation fell at 2 °C or below and nothing explains it, the page shows POSSIBLE SNOW, AWAITING CONFIRMATION.
  3. SLEET (a near miss) if the only wintry weather was sleet: a sleet code at St James's Park, or sleet (rain and snow mixed) reported nearby while St James's Park measured precipitation at 4 °C or below.
  4. NO SNOW otherwise. Hail (codes 19 to 21) is not snow, but it gets its own message.

Codes follow the Met Office DataHub Definition of Codes table. Every threshold is listed in CONFIG.md in the repository.

The yesterday verdict is final. The today so far status is provisional and updates every hour.

How the scene is chosen

The scene shows the most significant weather observed, in this order: snow, sleet or hail, thunder, heavy rain, light rain, fog or mist, strong wind, overcast, partly cloudy, then sunny (with hot and cold-and-bright variants). After sunset in London, the night version is shown. When no weather code is available, rain is worked out from measured precipitation, and the sky is shown as not reported rather than guessed.

Which millennial does what, where they stand and what passes by are chosen by a random number generator seeded with the date, so a given day always looks the same, but no two days look alike.

Limits

  • St James's Park is 600 metres from Trafalgar Square. Snow can be patchy, so the two places will not always match.
  • Observations are hourly. A brief flurry between two observation times can be missed.
  • Weather codes come from automatic sensors and can occasionally be wrong. The data is not the Met Office's quality-controlled climate record (that is published later, through MIDAS Open).
  • St James's Park has no present-weather sensor, so snow is usually confirmed with help from stations 13 to 22 km away. Very light snow that does not register in the St James's Park rain gauge can therefore be missed, and is shown as UNCONFIRMED if it was cold enough.
  • Automatic sensors sometimes report unidentified precipitation. This blocks a verdict only when St James's Park was wet or near freezing.
  • If data is missing or late, the page says so (SIGNAL LOST) instead of guessing.
